# Sources and confidence

A working bibliography for `index.html`. Dates are given as the consensus of the sources below, with disputes flagged.

## Primary and near-primary

- Vitruvius, *De Architectura* 10.6 (c. 27–22 BCE). Construction of the water screw: helical channels on a wooden shaft, Pythagorean 3-4-5 inclination, tread-powered. English: Thayer/LacusCurtius.
- Hero (Heron) of Alexandria, *Mechanica* (1st century CE). Screw as one of five machines that can “set a load in motion”; defined as an inclined plane wrapped around a cylinder; describes fabrication and a tap for internal threads.
- Pliny, *Natural History* 18.74.317. Screw-and-lever press.
- Diodorus Siculus, *Bibliotheca Historica* 1.34.2. Attributes the Nile irrigation screw to Archimedes.
- Gutenberg-era printing: no surviving original press. Earliest illustration of a press is 1499; wooden agricultural screw press is the accepted ancestor.
- *Das Mittelalterliche Hausbuch* (Waldburg-Wolfegg), plate often cited as 1483. Earliest known picture of a master-screw thread-cutting machine. Artist’s mechanical understanding is imperfect (Battison).
- Jacques Besson, *Theatrum Instrumentorum et Machinarum* (1569). Screw-cutting lathe.
- Galileo Galilei, *Le Meccaniche* (c. 1600). Dynamics of the simple machines, including the screw.
- Sabatino de Ursis and Xu Guangqi, *Taixi shuifa* / *Hydraulic Methods of the Far West* (1612). Archimedean screw introduced to China.
- Job and William Wyatt, British patent no. 751 (14 May 1760). Mechanised wood-screw manufacture.
- Joseph Whitworth, paper to the Institution of Civil Engineers (1841). 55° thread, standardised TPI by diameter.
- William Sellers, paper to the Franklin Institute (21 April 1864; committee report 15 December 1864). 60° thread, flat crest and root.
- ISO/R 68 (1958) basic profile; ISO/R 724 (1968) metric basic dimensions. ISO 68:1973 and later ISO 68-1.

## Secondary

- David Sim and Chris Legg, “The Production of Roman Metal Screw Threads — Extended Version,” *EXARC Journal* 2024/2. Roman V- and square threads; Herculaneum lamp; Silchester pointed iron wood screw; experimental production times.
- Stephanie Dalley and John Peter Oleson, “Sennacherib, Archimedes, and the Water Screw,” *Technology and Culture* 44.1 (2003). Competing cases for a 7th-century BCE Assyrian bronze water screw vs. Archimedes as inventor. **Disputed.**
- John Peter Oleson, work on the water screw (summarised in the Archimedes’ screw literature). No literary or archaeological evidence for the device before c. 250 BCE; Archimedes remains the conservative attribution.
- Edwin A. Battison, “Screw-Thread Cutting by the Master-Screw Method Since 1480,” Smithsonian (also Project Gutenberg eBook 31756). Hausbuch lathe; later master-screw machines.
- Witold Rybczynski, *One Good Turn: A Natural History of the Screwdriver and the Screw* (2000). Clock screws, wood screws, drive types. Extracts also at Lost Art Press.
- Conservators’ literature on American wood screws: Wyatt 1760 blunt machine-made screws; gimlet point c. 1834–1846 (Sloat & Springsteen, Whipple, J. T. Sloan USP 4864).
- ASME Landmark 234, “The United States Standard Screw Threads.” Sellers at the Franklin Institute; Navy and railroad adoption.
- National Screw Thread Commission / NBS reports. US thread history from 1864 through Unified.
- Fastener industry summaries (Bolt Science; Fastenerdata) for Whitworth, Sellers, Thury (47.5°), Loewenherz (53° 8′), Unified 1948, ISO metric.

## Drive types and later machinery

- P. L. Robertson, Canadian patent 1907 / US 1911 (manufacturing machine). Square internal drive, cold-formed.
- John P. Thompson, US patents 1,908,080 and 1,908,081 (1932), assigned to Henry F. Phillips. Phillips cruciform drive.
- Acme thread: proposed 1894 by Albert Man Powell (*American Machinist*, 24 January 1895); name from A. W. Handy of Acme Machinery Co. 29° trapezoidal power thread.
- Ball screws: patents from 1874; Cleveland Machine Screw Co. design documented 1898; WWII aircraft; GM Saginaw steering commercially from the 1950s.

## Confidence notes

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Archytas of Tarentum invented the screw c. 400 BCE | Traditional attribution. Evidence is thin; treat as legend with a plausible date for Greek mechanical interest. |
| Sennacherib’s bronze water screws at Nineveh (7th c. BCE) | Dalley’s reading of Akkadian *gišmahhu* / *alamittu*. Oleson and others are unconvinced. Flagged on the page as disputed. |
| Archimedes invented the water screw | Strongest conservative position. Egyptian-origin claims exist (Posidonius via later authors) but lack pre-250 BCE evidence. |
| Roman metal fastening screws were common | They existed (jewellery, lamps, surgical instruments, one Silchester wood screw). They were not a mass fastener. Nails and rivets did that job. |
| China independently invented the screw | No. Jesuit transmission in the 17th century. Needham’s “last of the simple machines” point stands. |
| Maudslay invented the screw-cutting lathe | He did not invent the idea. Senot (1795), Wilkinson (1798), Ramsden (1770), Besson (1569), and the Hausbuch (1483) precede him. He made the industrially practical combination that spread. |
| Gutenberg invented the screw press | He applied an existing agricultural / paper screw press to movable type. |

## Etymology

Romance words for the screw often come from Latin *vītis*, “vine” (French *vis*, Italian *vite*). English *screw* is from Old French *escroue* (the nut), possibly from Latin *scrōfa* “sow” (the spiral of a tail) or a Germanic root. The page uses the vine etymology as a genuine linguistic fossil of the geometry, not as proof of independent invention from plants.
